July 1961. A Hollywood film company drives up State Road 40 to Yankeetown, Florida, a fishing town of a few hundred people where the only road ends at the Gulf. The most famous young man in America is the star. You are the town.

End of the Road is a short, free documentary game about the six weeks when Follow That Dream was filmed on the Withlacoochee and at Bird Creek. First the council hears the company's requests: paint the road black so it looks new, turn the tip of Pumpkin Island into a white beach, find local help. Then come six weeks of crowds from all over Florida, summer thunderstorms, twenty townspeople fishing from the bridge with rubber fish, a reporter who wants to stand on the public road, and at the end a thatched shack that has to go somewhere.

The record, and what it does not say. Every choice shows what the reports say happened, and is marked as the record, a later account, a memory or a game assumption. Two voices are measured and neither wins: the town's quiet and the town's chance. The film is made whatever you decide; what changes is what Yankeetown lives through and keeps.

The star is not a character. Nobody puts words in his mouth, and no image, music or text from the film is used. The plates are a 1955 survey map of the end of the road, a 1950 photograph of the courthouse in Inverness and pictures of the places today. Links lead to the official recording of the song, videos of the locations and an interview with the twins from the film.

Do you remember that summer? If you or your family were there, the author would be glad to hear from you for a later version.
