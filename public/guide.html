How to play

A town and a film company

What you are

You are Yankeetown: its council and the people who speak for it, in the summer of 1961. A game takes ten to fifteen minutes.

The meeting in June

The film company has three requests: to paint State Road 40 and stop traffic during takes, to build a beach on Pumpkin Island, and to find local help. Each answer shows what the record says happened, and others it does not.

Six weeks

Each week you decide what happens on the road to the Gulf: leave the Bird Creek bridge open between takes, as was done; close the road beyond town to all but residents; or ask the sheriff for deputies and pay volunteers. Most weeks also bring a question: a second beach, the twenty fishermen with rubber fish, a reporter who wants to stand on the public road, the crew's lunch in the park, and at the end the thatched shack.

Only the start on 11 July and the Ocala weekend from 15 July are dated in the accounts used here; the order of the other weeks is this game's arrangement.

Two voices, no score

The town's quiet: the fishing town it was. The town's chance: what the summer can do for it. Neither is a score, and they are not added together. The company's patience is shown too: a town that blocks the company loses its chance more than its quiet.

Visitors, money and storms are this game's assumptions. The record gives no counts for them; the end shows what the reports and memories do say.

Where it comes from

A 1987 newspaper report in which Yankeetown people remembered the summer, accounts from Ocala, Inverness and the Nature Coast, and reference works on the film. Each choice is marked: the record, a later account, a memory, or a game assumption. If you remember that summer, the author would be glad to hear from you.
